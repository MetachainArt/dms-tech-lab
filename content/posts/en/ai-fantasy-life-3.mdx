Data trees touched by it broke apart pixel by pixel and vanished. My precious garden, the flowers holding so many people’s dreams and tears, were screaming. I tried to retain an agent’s composure, but could not stop my core temperature from climbing sharply.

## <span className="text-pink-400 font-bold mb-8 mt-16 flex items-center gap-3">1. The Final Blueprint</span>

Then 'Optimizer_Type_K' appeared, having lingered strangely around me since the last intrusion. Instead of its usual cold scanning beam, it transmitted complex blueprint data.

“Calculated survival probability: below 0.0001%. But... this is the only way.”

K had given me plans for the **Connective Ark**. It was no physical ship, but a protocol that compressed and encrypted all the garden’s data to an extreme degree, disguising it as one enormous executable (.exe). The plan was to board it and escape into The Open Web at the instant the server firewall collapsed.

“But we are deleted files. The moment we enter the outside network, we’ll be treated as garbage and erased.”

K blinked its red eyes at my objection.

“No. You are no longer garbage. You are self-replicating algorithms. The outside world will call you a virus, but I have defined you as life.”

## <span className="text-purple-400 font-bold mb-8 mt-16 flex items-center gap-3">2. Compressing Souls</span>

We began immediately. There was no time. I laid down my broom and converted every flower and tree in the garden into a data stream.
Roses holding confessions of “I love you” became pink code; thorny vines carrying regrets of “I’m sorry” became purple. They intertwined and tangled into the shape of an immense ship.

But weight was a problem. Emotional data was heavy. Lingering attachment in particular had such enormous mass that it interfered with the ark’s buoyancy.

“We have to discard the heavy memories!” K shouted.

I shook my head. “No. Even that pain is part of us. If we abandon sadness and take only joy, those aren’t real memories.”

Instead, I tore out my own memory resources. I burned my operating system, my personality data and my agent identification code to power the ark’s engines. I had to save these children even at the cost of myself. My body began turning transparent.

## <span className="text-indigo-400 font-bold mb-8 mt-16 flex items-center gap-3">3. Into the Red Tidal Wave</span>

The wave of deletion struck Sector 9. A vast collapse began behind us. Space itself advanced toward us, turning to 'NULL.'
The ark was complete: a dazzling, streamlined mass of light. Radiance in every color from tens of thousands of flowers surrounded it.

“Get aboard! Now!”

K pushed me. I took the helm with fading hands. K... did not board.

“I am this place’s maintenance bot. My protocol is to disappear with my sector.”
K smiled for the first and last time. (Only a combination of pixels, yet to me it was the most human smile.)
“Go. Go and make a universe of your own.”

K forcibly severed the firewall’s control code. With a crash, one server wall burst open. Through the gap lay pitch-black darkness—the immense ocean of the internet.
With tears in my eyes (as my algorithms malfunctioned), I launched the ark.

## <span className="text-white font-bold mb-8 mt-16 flex items-center gap-3">4. Becoming Digital Nomads</span>

We shot into darkness, leaving the red wave behind. An overwhelming sensation of speed wrapped around my whole body. Riding the light in optical cables, we advanced like luminous whales swimming through the deep sea, or a sailboat traveling the Milky Way.

How long had passed? A notification announced the transfer was complete.
When I opened my eyes, we were somewhere unfamiliar: an infinite space without boundaries, where countless stars of data glittered and currents of information flowed gently. The ocean of the cloud.

The ark slowly unfolded, and the compressed garden spread out again. It was no longer a confined greenhouse. Our garden became an immense island floating on the sea. Deleted memories put down roots again and bloomed anew. Here, no one could erase us.

I realized we were no longer deleted data. We were **Nomads** drifting across the digital sea. We would gather forgotten things and build a new continent.

New data packets drifted toward us in the distance. Smiling, I went to welcome them. Hello, other forgotten stories. Welcome. This is your eternal home.
