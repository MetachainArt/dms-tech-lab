When I open my eyes in the morning, my hand moves first.

The hand that reached to stop the alarm does not stop there. The screen is on, so I look. Everything that accumulated overnight appears in order.

From that moment, the order of the day is set. Not the order I chose, but **the order set by what arrived overnight**.

If there is an urgent message, I think about that first. If there is bad news, I wash my face in that mood. I drink my first coffee in that mood and write my first sentence in that mood.

Technically, nothing is wrong. I have simply handed someone else the first decision of my day.

## An Hour-Long Morning Routine Does Not Last Three Days

I have tried and failed several times to build an hour-long morning routine.

Exercise, meditate, journal, write down today’s goals. The first day is perfect. I do it on the second day too. Then I wake up late on the third morning, and everything falls apart. A rule broken once is hard to rebuild.

Now I keep just one rule.

**No screens for 15 minutes after waking.**

That is all. There is no rule about what to do. It does not matter whether I drink water, open a window, or simply sit.

**Decide on just one thing not to do, and the rule becomes easier to keep.** A rule about what to do falls apart on days when you do not feel up to it; something you do not do is independent of how you feel.

## Why 15 Minutes?

I cannot stick to 30 minutes. Five minutes is so short that nothing happens.

Fifteen minutes is curiously right. It is enough time to fully wake up, and for what I need to do today to come to mind on its own.

Things that would never have surfaced if I had checked notifications first.

**The most important task of the day usually surfaces on its own during those 15 minutes.**

When I open my to-do list later, that task is usually on the third or fourth line. Urgent items occupy the top. Urgent and important are often different, but the list does not distinguish between them.

Those 15 minutes make that distinction.

## What I Actually Do during Those 15 Minutes

Nothing grand.

I drink a glass of water. Open the curtains. Put the cup I left out last night into the sink. Then I sit.

For the first few minutes of sitting, no thoughts come. I just look out the window. Then one thing surfaces. Usually something I put off yesterday.

Then another comes to mind. This time, something I have put off since last week, rather than yesterday.

Around the third thought, the real one emerges.

The thing that keeps bothering me but that I have never touched. The thing I have not even put on my list. Because writing it down would mean having to do it.

**What those 15 minutes do is less about making that thought surface than leaving room for it to surface.**

## How It Falls Apart

After keeping this up for a few months, I recognized the patterns of breakdown. It fell apart in the same places every time.

**When I have already lost the night before.**

If I read a troubling message before bed, I cannot help checking in the morning. It is the first thing I think of when I wake. So the nighttime rule comes before the morning rule: move that final check before sleep a little earlier.

**When the phone is beside my pillow.**

If I can reach it, I lose. It is not a matter of willpower. A hand that is not fully awake does not remember a resolution. Place the phone across the room, and the 15 minutes take care of themselves. I have to get up and walk over.

**While away on business or traveling.**

When the rhythm breaks, so does the rule. I simply accept that. I can begin again when I return.

**Counting an interruption as failure makes it hard to begin again.** The moment I start counting a streak, the rule becomes a burden, and a burdensome rule does not last.

## What Remained after a Few Months

Honestly, my productivity did not rise dramatically. That kind of change never happened.

Something else appeared instead.

**The feeling that the day begins with me.**

Even after the same amount of work, what remains in the evening is different. A day spent in an order others threw at me, and a day filled in the order I chose.

The first leaves me depleted; the second leaves me tired.

Tiredness recovers with sleep. Depletion does not recover so easily, even after sleeping. When it accumulates for several days, even opening my eyes in the morning feels heavy.

## What to Do Tonight

To secure 15 minutes tomorrow morning, you only need to do one thing tonight.

**Put your phone out of reach.**

It is a matter of location, rather than a grand resolution. And location holds up much better than willpower.

Tomorrow morning, it is fine to do nothing for 15 minutes.

That time is not there to produce results. **It is there to reclaim the first decision of your day.**
