## <span className="text-emerald-400 font-bold mb-8 mt-16 flex items-center gap-3">🌱 A Small Resolution Takes Root</span>

I wanted to escape that weight. So I made one very small, modest resolution.

‘This month, let’s earn a certification.’

Even opening a book felt unfamiliar. The words I had kept at a distance behind the excuse of being busy did not come easily, and memorization was a difficult battle I had not faced in years. Yet strangely, whenever I discovered that I understood a little more today than yesterday, a small joy gathered like a drop of water in a corner of my heart.

And after a while, I held the certificate in my hands. To the world it was no more than a sheet of paper, but to me it was an immense and precious experience.

‘I can do things, too.’

For the first time, I understood: **a goal can become a reason to feel alive.**

## <span className="text-teal-400 font-bold mb-8 mt-16 flex items-center gap-3">⚓ An Anchor That Holds Me Steady</span>

From then on, I began designing my own days. There was no need for anything grand.
Read one page of a book, stretch for 5 minutes, write one blog post.

Those small pieces came together and changed me little by little. A goal was more than a task; it was a compass that gave my life **direction**.

A life with direction does not lose its way. Even after failing, you can rise again; even after pausing, you can return. Because you have a direction to turn toward again.

Now, when I wake in the morning, I first think of today’s goal. It is not an enormous plan that transforms the entire day. It is simply one small dot that keeps me from forgetting myself. As I move toward that dot, the day becomes a line, and those lines come together to draw ‘who I am today.’ A goal is a firm anchor that catches me whenever I waver.

## <span className="text-blue-400 font-bold mb-8 mt-16 flex items-center gap-3">🎁 A Way to Love Today</span>

Sometimes I ask myself:
‘What will doing this change? Do I really need to go this far?’

Whenever I do, I recall the flutter I felt on the day I earned that certification 10 years ago. Ever since, I have known: setting goals is not just about changing the future. It is **a way to love who I am right now more deeply**.

Making a plan for myself, acting on it for myself, and praising myself for following through. Could there be a more active expression of self-love?

So today, once again, I write a small goal in my notebook.
