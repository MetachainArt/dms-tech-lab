If you sell Notion templates or PDFs from your own sales page in Korea and wrote "no refunds," that line may not stop refunds as well as you expect. Korea's e-commerce consumer statute lets a seller block a buyer's withdrawal once delivery of digital content has begun. To do that, the seller must show that withdrawal is not possible and must also offer a trial in a way set by presidential decree. In 2022 the Korea Fair Trade Commission fined a streaming service after reading its refund wording as obstructing withdrawal, because the wording seemed to cover even unused passes. The notice boxes and sample wording below are DMS.Labs proposals built from the provisions. They are not a form the law requires.

Based on public materials checked on November 15, 2026. On the National Law Information Center I read Articles 2, 13, 17, 18 and 21 of the Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce (전자상거래 등에서의 소비자보호에 관한 법률, in force July 21, 2026) and Article 2, item 5 of the Framework Act on Cultural Industry Promotion (in force July 29, 2026). Article 21-2 of the Act's Enforcement Decree appeared in search results as quoted statute text, and the Commission's notice on exemption from mail-order business registration (in force April 5, 2022) I saw only as an excerpt from the same site. The Fair Trade Commission decision of February 18, 2022 (summary decision 약식2022-020) I read in full as posted on Casenote. I did not read the Commission's e-commerce consumer protection guidelines, the Korea Consumer Agency's dispute resolution standards, or any platform's refund policy. This is not legal advice, and whether your product counts as digital content, and when delivery "begins," can differ case by case. ## Do you have to refund a digital file after payment?

If delivery has not begun, you must accept withdrawal. Once it has begun, you can block withdrawal only if you have taken the required steps.

Article 17(1) lets a consumer who bought from a mail-order seller withdraw within 7 days of receiving the written contract terms. If the goods or services were supplied later than that document, the 7 days run from receipt or from the start of supply. Article 17(2), item 5 lists, among the cases where a consumer cannot withdraw against the seller's will, the case where supply of a service or of "digital content" under the Framework Act on Cultural Industry Promotion has begun. That Act defines digital content as characters, figures, images, video and similar material or information produced or processed in digital form, so a PDF or a Notion template plausibly falls inside it. Whether your own product does is not something I can decide here.

The proviso of Article 17(2) matters most. If the seller has not taken the measures in paragraph 6, the consumer can withdraw even in the item 5 case. For digital content, paragraph 6 asks the seller to state that withdrawal is not possible and to provide a trial product in the way the decree sets, so that the right to withdraw is not obstructed. The limit comes from having both of those in place. The words "no refunds" supply neither.

Two things remain even when both are in place. Under paragraph 3, if the content differs from what was displayed or advertised, or was delivered differently from the contract, the consumer can withdraw within 3 months of receiving it and within 30 days of learning, or being able to learn, of the difference. And when there is a dispute over whether and when the contract was made, or whether and when the goods were supplied, the seller must prove it (paragraph 5). That is why order records and file delivery records are worth keeping.

| Situation | What the provisions say |
| --- | --- |
| Paid, but delivery of the file has not begun | Item 5 limits withdrawal only once delivery has begun. Before that, it does not apply, and the 7-day withdrawal in paragraph 1 stays open. |
| Delivery has begun, and you gave the notice and a trial | Under item 5, you can block withdrawal against your will. |
| Delivery has begun, but the notice or the trial is missing | Under the proviso of paragraph 2, the consumer can withdraw. |
| A contract split into installments or files, for the part not yet delivered | If the content is divisible, the part whose delivery has not begun cannot be limited. |
| A file that differs from the display or advertisement, or from the contract | Under paragraph 3, withdrawal is open for 3 months from receipt and 30 days from discovery. |

When withdrawal happens, a seller who supplied digital content must refund the payment within 3 business days of the day the consumer withdrew, and owes late-payment damages if it is late (Article 18(2)). The consumer does not have to return digital content already received (paragraph 1), and for a withdrawal under the 7-day rule the seller cannot charge a penalty or claim damages (paragraph 9).

The provisions do not say whether delivery "begins" at payment, when you send the download link, or when the buyer actually downloads the file. I do not settle that here. What I propose is that the seller states the starting point in the notice.

## Why can 'no refunds' become a problem?

If the wording reads as blocking more than the provisions allow, the wording itself can become a prohibited act. Article 21(1), item 1 prohibits informing consumers of false or exaggerated facts, or using deceptive means, to obstruct withdrawal.

The Fair Trade Commission's decision of February 18, 2022 (summary decision 약식2022-020, Contents Wavve Corporation, case number 2019전자2265) applied that provision to wording. The sales screen for a monthly pass said, in Korean, that a pass that has been purchased and used would not be cancelled or refunded. The customer center said that online content is considered used once opened, so a refund is impossible. The Commission found that the wording included the meaning that withdrawal is flatly impossible even when the consumer has not used the content, and treated it as informing consumers of false or exaggerated facts.

It did not rule that the company had given no accurate notice at all. A screen where the customer entered a payment method said that an unused product could be withdrawn within 7 days of purchase. The Commission reasoned that a customer who uses a pass after buying it is unlikely to look at the payment-step notice again, while the customer center's notice said withdrawal after purchase was impossible, so a consumer could well hesitate to withdraw. The result was an order not to repeat the conduct and a fine of 3 million won, and the case also covered missing business identity information on the site. The company admitted the facts and the violation and accepted the opinion, and a footnote in the decision shows that its corrected terms limited withdrawal where the content had been provided by streaming or download and the member had begun to use it.

That case involved a large operator's monthly pass, not an individual seller's PDF. I cannot say the same wording would draw the same penalty on a small file sale. It is useful as a view of how the Commission read wording: does it seem to block even unused products, and is one accurate notice before payment enough?

| Common wording | How it can read | Wording that follows the provisions (DMS.Labs proposal) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No refunds | Withdrawal is impossible even before you get the file | Before delivery of the file begins, you can withdraw within 7 days. Once delivery has begun, withdrawal is limited. |
| Opening the download link counts as use | Sending the link and receiving the file are not separated | Delivery begins when you first download the file from the link in the payment confirmation email. |
| No refunds for a change of mind | Seems to block withdrawal even for a file that differs from the description | If you receive a file that differs from the description, you can withdraw within 3 months of receipt and within 30 days of learning of the difference. |

The starting point in the second row is something a seller chooses, not a definition in the provisions. I could not check whether a dispute would accept it as written.

## What must a sales page state?

Article 13 sets the items, and they should be visible before checkout. Paragraph 1 requires the display and advertisement to include the business name and representative, address, phone number and email, and the registration number and details for confirming it under Article 12. Paragraph 2 requires the seller to display or announce the transaction terms so the consumer understands them before contracting, and, once the contract is made, to hand over a written document with those terms by the time the goods or services are supplied.

The items tied to refunds are these. Item 5 covers the time limit, method and effect of withdrawal, including any form needed. Item 6 covers exchange, return and warranty, and the conditions and procedure for refunds. Item 7 covers the technical requirements for transmitting or installing goods supplied electronically. Item 8 covers handling of complaints and disputes, and item 9 covers the terms of the transaction. Folded into five boxes for a sales page, they look like this.

| Box | What to write | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. When the file is delivered | When and where the link goes after payment, and what counts as the start of delivery | Article 17(2), item 5. The starting-point wording is a DMS.Labs proposal |
| 2. Withdrawal period and method | Within 7 days, how to apply, what to include in the request | Article 13(2), item 5; Article 17(1) |
| 3. Notice that withdrawal is limited | That withdrawal is limited once delivery has begun, shown close to the pay button | Article 17(6). The text says a place the consumer can easily see; "close to the pay button" is a DMS.Labs proposal |
| 4. Trial | At least one of a preview, time-limited use, or a trial version | Enforcement Decree Article 21-2 |
| 5. Refund and exceptions | Refund within 3 business days of withdrawal; 3 months and 30 days for a file that differs from the description | Article 18(2); Article 17(3) |

The five boxes are DMS.Labs's attempt at turning the provisions into a page, and I did not test their effect. Filling every box means the notice follows the provisions. It does not mean disputes will not arise.

## How do you offer a trial?

Article 21-2 of the Enforcement Decree sets four methods and says to use at least one. Partial use means offering part of the digital content as a preview or sample listening. Time-limited use means setting a period of use. A trial version means supplying a version with only some functions. The last method, providing information about the digital content, is allowed only when the first three are hard to provide.

| Method in the decree | How it might look for a Notion template or PDF (DMS.Labs proposal) |
| --- | --- |
| Partial use | A sample PDF with the first few pages, a public page showing one screen of the template |
| Time-limited use | A read-only link that opens for a set period |
| Trial version | A stripped-down template with fewer functions |
| Information | Page count, file format, list of included items, screenshots. Only when the first three are hard to provide |

The provisions do not say how much of a trial is enough, and I do not set a standard either. If you plan to limit withdrawal after delivery begins, the first thing to check is that this box is not empty.

## If you have not registered as a mail-order seller, are refund rules off the table?

Exemption from registration and the reach of the refund rules read as separate questions. The proviso of Article 12(1) exempts sellers whose transaction count, volume and similar measures are at or below a Commission-set standard. Article 2 of the Commission's notice lists, as cases where registration can be skipped, fewer than 50 mail-order transactions in the previous year or being a simplified-tax business under the VAT Act. Withdrawals do not count toward the number of transactions.

Article 2, item 3 of the Act, however, defines a mail-order seller as a person who engages in mail-order sales as a business, or one who carries out the work under an agreement with such a person, and does not make registration a condition. In the provisions I read, I found no basis for saying that falling inside the exemption also removes you from Article 17. Whether someone sells "as a business" is a judgment I do not make here. Before you leave refund wording out because your sales are small, check your own case with your local government or the Fair Trade Commission's guidance. If you sell through an open market or store platform, the platform's refund policy will overlap with yours, and I did not read any platform's policy.

## What more does a subscription need?

Article 13(6) has a separate rule for payments that repeat, such as a monthly subscription. When a recurring charge rises, or something supplied free turns into a paid recurring charge, the seller must obtain the consumer's consent beforehand, within a period set by decree, covering the date, the price before and after, and the payment method, and must explain how to cancel or terminate and the effect of doing so. The paragraph was added on February 13, 2024. I did not read the decree, so I do not state the length of the period.

The provisions do not say whether a subscription counts as divisible digital content, and the decision above dealt only with the wording on a monthly pass, so it does not answer that either. If you sell subscriptions, my suggestion is to add to box 1 what is supplied in each billing period.
