## Where should you check first?

The video shows how to sign in to Flow with a Google account, then open the profile at the top right to view the remaining credits and refresh time. Knowing your balance before you begin helps you judge whether you have room for another attempt. Start by checking whether the free allowance actually appears in your account.

Next come the new project settings. The video explains that the credit cost appears when you select video, aspect ratio, model, resolution, and duration. It emphasizes setting the number of outputs to one. Rather than estimating the cost from the model name alone, it is safer to check whether you have selected a different resolution or duration, even with the same model.

In your own practice, distinguish between one desired result and one generation request. Generating again because you dislike the result counts as another request, so a suggested approach is not to allocate all your remaining credits to the first attempt. This is a planning guideline that allows for retries, not a saving demonstrated or guaranteed by the video.

## How should you interpret the figures in the video?

The description of the October 12, 2026 video lists Omni 1.1 Flash at seven credits for 720p and four seconds, and four credits for 360p and four seconds. Veo 3.1 Lite at 720p and eight seconds is listed at 10 credits. Assuming 50 credits per day, the video gives daily totals of seven, 12, and five videos respectively. These figures are examples under the conditions at that time.

Those daily generation counts assume that every request uses the same settings. They no longer apply unchanged if you switch the model, duration, or number of outputs along the way. To check the current cost, look again at the credit charge displayed immediately before you run a generation, rather than relying on the figures in this article.

The video also stated at the time that unused credits could not be carried over, that non-subscribing accounts might face generation restrictions between 11 p.m. and 2 a.m. Korea time, and that watermarking and free 1080p upscaling were subject to restrictions in Korea. These are not newly verified statements of current policy, so check your account guidance and the relevant feature displays before use.

## What should you check after generation?

The video shows how to download the completed video at its original size through Download in the three-dot menu. However, a finished generation is not the same as getting the scene you wanted. The creator says the bottle changed shape during a rotation despite an instruction to preserve its shape, and urges viewers to review the results.

The following are suggested checks before downloading. Look for changes in the bottle's silhouette at the beginning, middle, and end, and confirm the desired aspect ratio and duration. Checking whether the watermark is acceptable for your intended use can also help reduce the chance of downloading a file that turns out to be difficult to use.

[Watch the actual process in this video](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/nFEhEZGt_5g)

The first step toward making good use of free credits is a habit of checking the conditions, rather than a trick for maximizing the number of generations. Review your balance and refresh time, generation settings and credit charge, and the shape in the finished video, in that order. Treat this Short's figures as a record from October 12, and decide whether to generate today based on the information displayed in your own account.
