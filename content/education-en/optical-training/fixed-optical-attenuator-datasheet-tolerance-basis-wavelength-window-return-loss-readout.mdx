The tolerance of a fixed optical attenuator is printed as a step table of plus or minus dB, as a percentage, or as one line per part number, depending on the document, so the same 10 dB part can differ by anything from ±0.5 dB to ±1.5 dB in the worst case. Before choosing an attenuator, copy line by line the tolerance steps and their basis, whether the value includes connector loss, whether the wavelength window covers the PON wavelengths in use, and the return loss and maximum input. In the 11 datasheets read here, the tolerance of a 20 dB part ranges from ±1.0 dB to ±2.8 dB, and in a bidirectional path the same attenuator also reduces both the downstream and the upstream signal, which has to enter the calculation.

Based on documents checked on November 24, 2026.

## Learning objectives

- Write whether the tolerance line is a step table of plus or minus dB, a percentage or a per-part line, and calculate the worst-case attenuation range for the value you plan to use.

- Check line by line whether the attenuation value includes connector loss, whether the wavelength window covers 1310, 1490 and 1577 nm, and whether return loss is given per polish type.

- Include the fact that an attenuator reduces both downstream and upstream signals, and calculate the overload margin with the worst-case attenuation.

## How is the tolerance of a fixed optical attenuator printed in datasheets?

Tolerance is printed in four ways. The first is a separate plus or minus dB line for each attenuation step, used by Lightel, Go4Fiber, OMC and Huber+Suhner. The second is plus or minus dB for the low steps and a percentage for the high steps, used by FS and Optech. The third is a single ±1.5 dB line without steps, used by PrecisionOT. The fourth is a tolerance for each part number at Thorlabs, and a table by grade and wavelength band at Gooch & Housego (G&H). Lines that look alike do not share the same basis.

| Document | Attenuation range | Tolerance line | What to check when reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FS LC/UPC datasheet (two files) | 0–30 dB | 1–9 dB ±0.5 dB, 10–25 dB ±10% | No tolerance line for 26–30 dB, and the document does not say whether the percentage refers to the dB value |
| Optech 19V.A | 1–30 dB | 1–5 dB ±0.5 dB, 6–30 dB ±10% | Same percentage notation with no basis explained |
| Lightel V3 (March 2023) | 1–30 dB | 1–9 dB ±0.5, 10–14 dB ±1.0, 15–30 dB ±1.5 | Three steps whose boundaries differ from FS |
| Go4Fiber 2021 v2 | 1–25 dB | 0–15 dB ±1.0, 16–25 dB ±1.5 | ±1.0 dB even at 5 dB |
| OMC 2018 v1.0 (LC in-line) | 1–30 dB | 1–10 dB at most 0.5 dB, 11–30 dB at most 1 dB | Printed as a limit without a sign, with insertion loss on a separate line |
| Huber+Suhner fixed in-line (single mode) | 1–30 dB | 1–10 dB at most 0.5 dB, 11–30 dB at most 1.0 dB | The multimode lines are wider: at most 1.0 dB for 1–10 dB and 2.0 dB for 11–25 dB |
| PrecisionOT (revised September 2023) | 1–20 dB | ±1.5 dB | No steps, so a 1 dB part has the same width |
| Thorlabs product page | 3–25 dB | 3 dB ±0.5, 5 dB ±0.5, 10 dB ±0.7 (FC/PC) and ±1.0 (FC/APC), 15 dB ±1.0, 20 dB ±1.0, 25 dB ±1.3 (FC/PC) and ±1.5 (FC/APC) | Separate lines per polish type, and the APC line is said to include key alignment variability |
| G&H PEC 0124 Issue 3 (June 2024) | 1–20 dB | Tables by grade A and B and by C band and C+L band (10 dB grade A: ±0.6 in the C band, ±0.9 in the C+L band) | The tolerance is tied to a wavelength band |

The percentage notation can be read in two ways because the documents do not state a basis. Read as 10% of the dB value, a 20 dB part is ±2.0 dB. Read as 10% of the transmitted power, it is about ±0.4 dB at every value, so the tolerance would shrink when going from 9 dB to 10 dB, which does not match the tendency of the other documents that grow with the step. The dB-value reading is therefore the more natural one. Corning's 754 series specification (revision of September 7, 2017), seen only as a search excerpt, prints more than 5 dB as ±10% of dB value, which supports the dB-value reading, but that document was blocked for download and was not opened, so it is used as a clue only. Read the FS and Optech percentages as a dB-value basis too, and record that the documents do not define it (DMS interpretation).

## How far can the worst case of the same 10 dB part differ?

Applying each document's tolerance line to a 10 dB and a 20 dB part gives different worst-case attenuation ranges. At 10 dB, OMC and Huber+Suhner are the narrowest at ±0.5 dB, Thorlabs FC/PC is ±0.7 dB, FS, Optech, Lightel, Go4Fiber and Thorlabs FC/APC are ±1.0 dB, and PrecisionOT is ±1.5 dB. At 20 dB, OMC, Huber+Suhner and Thorlabs are ±1.0 dB, Lightel, Go4Fiber and PrecisionOT are ±1.5 dB, and FS and Optech are ±2.0 dB. G&H keeps the wavelength band narrow and, depending on the grade, prints ±1.4 dB (grade A) to ±2.4 dB (grade B) in the C band and ±2.4 dB to ±2.8 dB in the C+L band at 20 dB.

![Bars placing the worst-case attenuation range of 10 dB and 20 dB fixed attenuators side by side by document](/images/education/optical-training/fixed-optical-attenuator-datasheet-tolerance-basis-wavelength-window-return-loss-readout/bars-en.svg)

- Each bar applies the tolerance line of a document to an attenuation value of 10 dB or 20 dB

- The dark dashed line is the nominal value, and a wider bar needs a larger margin set aside in the design

- Percentage lines were read as 10% of the dB value, and for G&H only the C band values are drawn

Documents that use the same value still weigh the numbers differently. For the same 5 dB part, FS, Optech, Lightel, OMC, Huber+Suhner and Thorlabs print within ±0.5 dB, Go4Fiber prints ±1.0 dB and PrecisionOT prints ±1.5 dB. If a design only names the attenuation value, any of them looks like the same part, so copy the complete tolerance line into the purchase specification and the receiving record.

## Does the value include connector loss and changes with wavelength and temperature?

Most documents do not answer this question. Three do. Thorlabs states that the attenuation in its table does not include insertion loss and was measured with a free-space detector, so extra loss can appear when the part is placed between fiber-coupled components. G&H also notes that the attenuation in its specification table does not include connector losses. OMC gives a single-mode insertion loss of at most 0.20 dB on a line separate from the attenuation value. FS, Optech, Lightel, Go4Fiber, PrecisionOT and Huber+Suhner do not state it, so it cannot be known whether the 10 dB on the label is the total added loss when the part is inserted.

Temperature and wavelength changes are also lines separate from the tolerance. G&H prints wavelength dependent loss (WDL), polarization dependent loss (PDL) and temperature dependent loss (TDL) next to the tolerance, and for the 10 dB grade A in the C band they read at most 0.13 dB, 0.10 dB and 0.13 dB. Optech prints a 0.6 dB change between −40°C and 80°C, which is larger than the ±0.5 dB tolerance of the 1–5 dB step in the same document. The document does not say at which temperature the tolerance applies, so read it as a room-temperature value and set aside a separate margin for temperature (DMS interpretation). OMC prints an insertion loss of at most 0.2 dB from −40°C to 85°C and a change of at most 0.1 dB under 10–55 Hz vibration for 2 hours.

## Does the wavelength window cover 1310, 1490 and 1577 nm of PON?

Whether the printed window contains the PON wavelengths differs by document. GPON uses 1480–1500 nm downstream, and XGS-PON uses 1575–1580 nm downstream and 1260–1280 nm upstream (G.984.2 and G.9807.1 Amendment 1, checked in the preceding lesson). The table copies each window as printed and marks the judgement as DMS interpretation. Some documents say that performance outside the specified window is not guaranteed, and Thorlabs is one of them.

| Document | Printed wavelength window | 1310 nm | 1490 nm (GPON downstream) | 1577 nm (XGS-PON downstream) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FS and PrecisionOT | 1260–1620 nm | Inside | Inside | Inside |
| Go4Fiber and Thorlabs | 1240–1620 nm | Inside | Inside | Inside |
| Optech | 1200–1600 nm or 1310 and 1550 nm, by choice | Inside | Inside for the 1200–1600 version, not stated for the 1310 and 1550 version | Inside for the 1200–1600 version, not stated for the 1310 and 1550 version |
| Lightel | Centers 1310 and 1550 nm, bandwidth ±40 nm | Inside | Between the two windows, not stated (DMS interpretation) | Inside (when read as 1510–1590 nm) |
| OMC | 1310 and 1550 nm | Inside | Not stated | Not stated |
| Huber+Suhner | 1310 and 1550 nm (±10) | Inside | Not stated | Not stated |
| G&H | C 1528–1563, L 1570–1605, C+L 1528–1605, S 1425–1500 nm | No such band | Only the S band part | Only the L band and C+L band parts |

If the Lightel centers of 1310 and 1550 nm with ±40 nm are read as two windows, they are 1270–1350 nm and 1510–1590 nm, so the GPON downstream at 1490 nm falls between them. The document does not say whether the region between the windows is covered, so to use this part on GPON, choose another datasheet or measure the attenuation at 1490 nm. The G&H C band part prints only 1528–1563 nm and falls on none of the three PON wavelengths. Nothing was tested this time; only the windows in the documents were compared.

## How are return loss and maximum input read?

First look at whether return loss is printed per polish type (UPC, APC). FS, Optech, Lightel and OMC print the same UPC at least 50 dB and APC at least 60 dB, PrecisionOT prints UPC more than 55 dB and APC more than 60 dB, and Go4Fiber prints UPC at least 55 dB and APC at least 65 dB. Thorlabs prints at least 40 dB for FC/PC and at least 60 dB for FC/APC, so its FC/PC line is 10 dB below the UPC 50 dB of the other documents. G&H prints 55 dB for the fused component with the input port P1 as the reference and no polish distinction. Putting a part that has only a UPC line into a path that needs APC changes the reflection condition, so copy the polish type and the return loss into the same cell.

Maximum input is 200 mW (about +23 dBm) at FS, Optech, Lightel, PrecisionOT and OMC, and 1 W (+30 dBm) at Thorlabs. G&H and Huber+Suhner have no maximum input line. Go4Fiber lists high power endurance as a feature but gives no number. Optech prints a maximum of 200 mW and, next to it, a durability well over 100 mW, and the document does not explain how to use the two lines together. As seen in the preceding lesson, the average output of PON modules reaches +7 dBm (about 5 mW) for a GPON C+ OLT and +11 dBm (about 13 mW) for an XGS-PON E2, so this line seldom becomes the limit, but copy it for paths that contain amplifiers.

## How do you find mismatches inside a document and between editions?

This material had five places where values disagree inside one document or between two files of the same product. The documents alone cannot tell which side is right, so record both values.

| Document | Where it disagrees | The two values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two files of the FS LC/UPC datasheet | Same product name but different operating temperature and return loss notation | −40–75°C against −25–70°C, and the second file prints UPC 50 and APC 60 without the at-least sign |
| FS LC/UPC datasheet | Range of the loss scope and of the tolerance lines | Scope is 0–30 dB, tolerance lines stop at 25 dB |
| G&H PEC 0124 Issue 2 (September 2017) and Issue 3 (June 2024) | Polarization dependent loss of grade A at 1–3 dB | 0.80 dB in Issue 2, 0.08 dB in Issue 3 |
| PrecisionOT | Part number example and its description | The number of PRE-ATLC-UPC-7-M3 says 7 but the description says 20 dB |
| PrecisionOT | The 20 dB line of the attenuation percentage table | The table prints 97.6, the calculated value is 99.0% |

The 0.80 dB of G&H Issue 2 is larger than the 0.2 dB tolerance on the same line and looks like a misprint, but the document does not announce a correction, so it is only noted as an inference. The PrecisionOT conversion table matches the calculation for most rows, such as 21% at 1 dB, 50% at 3 dB and 90% at 10 dB, and only the 20 dB row differs. A small misprint like this does not mean the other lines of the datasheet are wrong, but it is a reason to record the edition number and date when receiving a table and to calculate one or two representative values yourself. Under the rule against contacting manufacturers, the misprints were not queried and both values are written as they are.

## How do you put an attenuator value into a field overload calculation?

The FOA guide says to place the attenuator near the receiver and to choose the attenuation so that the received power sits near the middle of the receiver operating range. In its example, with a 0 dBm transmitter and a receiver overload of −15 dBm, the cable plant and attenuator must add up to at least 15 dB, and it recommends 20 dB. The example uses a single transmitter output, but datasheets print a minimum and a maximum, so use the maximum output for the overload check and the minimum output for the sensitivity check (DMS interpretation). In a PON, one attenuator also reduces both the downstream and the upstream signal.

The case here is a hypothetical teaching calculation that uses the G.984.2 values confirmed in the preceding [PON optical module lesson](/en/education/optical-training/pon-optical-module-datasheet-launch-power-sensitivity-overload-ber-basis-readout); it is not a test of a real site or product. Assume a path loss of 14 dB including splitters and splices, a C+ OLT (launch +3 to +7 dBm, receiver overload −12 dBm) and an ONU with launch +0.5 to +5 dBm and receiver overload −8 dBm. Without an attenuator the downstream ONU receive reaches −7 dBm, 1 dB over the overload, and the upstream OLT receive reaches −9 dBm, 3 dB over the overload. The upstream exceeds more, so the attenuation is decided by the upstream.

| Attenuator (tolerance line) | Worst-case attenuation | Downstream ONU maximum receive (overload −8) | Upstream OLT maximum receive (overload −12) | Judgement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| None | 0 dB | −7 dBm (1 dB over) | −9 dBm (3 dB over) | Over in both directions |
| 3 dB ±0.5 | 2.5 dB | −9.5 dBm (1.5 dB margin) | −11.5 dBm (0.5 dB over) | Upstream is over |
| 3 dB ±1.5 | 1.5 dB | −8.5 dBm (0.5 dB margin) | −10.5 dBm (1.5 dB over) | Upstream is over |
| 5 dB ±0.5 | 4.5 dB | −11.5 dBm (3.5 dB margin) | −13.5 dBm (1.5 dB margin) | Passes in both directions |
| 5 dB ±1.5 | 3.5 dB | −10.5 dBm (2.5 dB margin) | −12.5 dBm (0.5 dB margin) | Passes with a small margin |

On the sensitivity side, with the same 5 dB ±0.5 part, the minimum downstream receive is +3 − 14 − 5.5 = −16.5 dBm and the minimum upstream receive is +0.5 − 14 − 5.5 = −19 dBm, more than 13 dB above the sensitivities of −30 dBm and −32 dBm. The calculation is a plain subtraction without insertion loss of the part, splice loss or optical path penalty. After installation, therefore, measure the received power with an optical power meter, and do not use the ±3 dB accuracy of DDM (digital diagnostic monitoring) to judge a margin of about 1 dB (DMS interpretation).

![Order for reading a fixed optical attenuator datasheet](/images/education/optical-training/fixed-optical-attenuator-datasheet-tolerance-basis-wavelength-window-return-loss-readout/concept-en.svg)

- Write the tolerance steps and basis first, and calculate the worst-case attenuation as the value minus the tolerance

- Check whether connector loss is included and the wavelength window, then apply the PON wavelengths

- Calculate both downstream and upstream, and measure with an optical power meter after installation

The material has limits as well. The bodies of Telcordia GR-910-CORE and IEC 61753-021-2 were not read, and FS states GR-910 compliance without a test report. The Corning 754 series specification was blocked with a 403 error and is not counted as a source, and the figures of the secondary selection guide were used only for confirmation. All values are published by the manufacturers and were not tested here. Whether each datasheet is the current revision and matches the model sold today was not confirmed.

## Checklist

- Did you write whether the tolerance line is a step table of plus or minus dB, a percentage or a per-part line, and calculate the worst-case attenuation at the value you use?

- Did you confirm in the document whether the attenuation value includes connector loss, and record it as unconfirmed if it is not stated?

- Did you check whether the wavelength window covers the PON wavelengths in use (1310, 1490, 1577 nm), and write a measurement plan if they fall between or outside the windows?

- Did you copy return loss and polish type (UPC, APC) into the same cell and confirm that it matches the polish type of the path?

- Did you calculate the overload margin in both directions with the worst-case attenuation, and record the edition number and date of the datasheet?

## Review questions

### 1. A datasheet prints ±0.5 dB for 1–9 dB and ±10% for 10–25 dB. What worst-case attenuation range do you calculate for a 20 dB part?

Answer and explanation: Reading the percentage as 10% of the dB value gives ±2.0 dB, so 18.0 to 22.0 dB. If the document does not state the basis of the percentage, record this reading, and use 18.0 dB as the minimum attenuation in the overload check.

### 2. The wavelength line of a datasheet is centers of 1310 nm and 1550 nm with a bandwidth of ±40 nm. Do you judge that it can be used at the GPON downstream of 1490 nm?

Answer and explanation: No. Read as 1270–1350 nm and 1510–1590 nm, 1490 nm lies between the two windows, and the document does not say that the region between them is guaranteed. Choose a datasheet that prints a continuous window (for example 1260–1620 nm), or measure the attenuation at 1490 nm yourself (DMS interpretation).

### 3. With a 14 dB path loss, a C+ OLT and an ONU launching +5 dBm, is a 3 dB ±0.5 attenuator enough?

Answer and explanation: It is not enough. The downstream keeps a margin of 1.5 dB, but the upstream OLT receive becomes −11.5 dBm at the worst-case attenuation of 2.5 dB, which is 0.5 dB over the C+ OLT overload of −12 dBm. A 5 dB ±0.5 part gives −13.5 dBm upstream, a 1.5 dB margin. The calculation is hypothetical, so confirm with an optical power meter after installation (DMS interpretation).
